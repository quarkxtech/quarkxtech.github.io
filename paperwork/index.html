Paperwork automation

The paperwork files itself.

It reads what arrives, checks it against what you know, files it where it belongs, and puts the record to work on replies, purchasing, suppliers and forecasts.

On-premise. Documents never leave the building Exports to SAP, your ERP, or Excel Escalates when unsure, to a person

The problem

Someone is retyping a document right now.

In most manufacturers and trading companies, a document arrives, a person retypes it, and the system treats the result as fact.

The work is invisible until it fails

Nobody counts the hours spent keying invoices, delivery orders and packing lists. The cost surfaces when a wrong number reaches a customer or an auditor.

It does not scale with the business

Twice the volume needs nearly twice the people, and people need training, leave cover and somewhere to sit.

Generic tools miss your documents

Off-the-shelf capture is trained on tidy invoices. Real paperwork is inconsistent, handwritten, and frequently a photograph of a fax.

Your documents are commercially sensitive

Pricing, customers, volumes and margins are all in there. That alone rules out a cloud service for many operators.

What it does

One pipeline in. One memory. Every workflow out.

Reading documents is the front door. Everything that arrives becomes a validated record, and the workflows that run the business draw from it, each gated by a person until you decide otherwise.

The failure that matters is not a document the system cannot read, but one it reads wrongly and files with confidence. So validation is the product, and the lanes switch on one at a time, each proven on your real documents before the next.

How it is built

Inside your building, in formats you keep.

Deployment
Runs on a machine inside your network. Documents are not uploaded to a third-party service.
Intake
Scanned paper, emailed attachments, photographs taken on the floor. Handwriting and poor scans are expected, not edge cases.
Validation
Extracted values are checked against your master data and for internal consistency, such as line items reconciling to totals.
Escalation
Anything unconfirmed is queued for a person, with the source document and the field in question side by side.
Export
Into SAP, your ERP, or Excel, through documented interfaces, never database writes behind your vendor's back.
Your records
Extracted data is written to open formats as part of normal running: files you control, independently of us.

Scope

It starts with one document type.

Not the whole back office at once. The first engagement takes one high-volume document, proves the accuracy on your real paperwork, and earns the right to the next.

Accuracy on your documents

A sample batch of your actual paperwork, reported as what the system gets right, gets wrong, and refuses to guess. You see the numbers before committing.

Integration into a test system

Data goes into a sandbox copy of your system of record, reconciled against what your team would have entered by hand.

Live running and handover

Error logging, an escalation queue your staff own, and training. The people doing this work today become the people supervising it.

What we build and harden for one operator is adapted for the next with the same problem: it arrives proven, and it arrives fast.

Beyond the filing

The paperwork is where it starts, not where it ends.

Once documents flow through one pipeline, the work that follows can ride the same rails: read, checked, and escalated to a person when unsure.

Supplier comparison
Quotations lined up on like terms, so choosing a vendor starts from the numbers, not from whoever formatted their PDF best.
Drafted replies
Routine correspondence drafted from the documents at hand, approved by a person before sending. Nothing leaves the building on its own.
Inventory signals
Stock estimates from the sales and delivery history already in the pipeline, so purchasing sees what is running out before the shelf does.
Patterns over time
A record you can ask questions: which supplier slips, which customer pays late, which product moves. On your data, inside your walls.

The staircase

You cannot put AI on paper.

You cannot skip a step. And AI cannot read a logbook in a drawer. Wherever your operation stands on the staircase, the pipeline starts there.
