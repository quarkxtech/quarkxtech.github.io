Paperwork automation

The paperwork files itself.

This system turns the paperwork that runs your operation into one that runs it with you: reading what arrives, checking it against what you know, filing it where it belongs, and putting the record to work on replies, purchasing, suppliers and forecasts.

On-premise. Documents never leave the building Exports to SAP, your ERP, or Excel In scoping with a Malaysian trading company

The problem

Someone is retyping a document right now.

In most traditional manufacturers and trading companies, the operational record is assembled by hand. A document arrives, a person reads it, and a person types it into a system that then treats it as fact.

The work is invisible until it fails

Nobody tracks how many hours go into keying delivery orders, invoices, packing lists and shipping documents. The cost only becomes visible when a wrong number reaches a customer or an auditor.

It does not scale with the business

Twice the volume needs close to twice the people, and those people need training, cover during leave, and somewhere to sit.

Generic tools miss your documents

Off-the-shelf capture products are trained on tidy invoices. Real operational paperwork is inconsistent, supplier-specific, sometimes handwritten, and frequently a photograph of a fax.

Your documents are commercially sensitive

Pricing, customers, volumes and margins are all in there. For many operators that alone rules out sending them to a cloud service.

What it does

One pipeline in. One memory. Every workflow out.

Reading documents is the front door. The design is the whole house: everything that arrives becomes a validated operational record, and the workflows that run the business draw from that record, each one gated by a person until you decide otherwise.

The failure that matters is not a document the system cannot read. It is one it reads wrongly and files with confidence. So the validation layer is the product, and the escalation path is not an afterthought. An engagement switches these lanes on one at a time, each proven on your real documents before the next.

How it is built

Inside your building, in formats you keep.

Deployment
Runs on a machine inside your network. Documents are not uploaded to a third-party service.
Intake
Scanned paper, emailed attachments, and photographs taken on the floor. Handwriting and poor scans are expected rather than treated as edge cases.
Validation
Extracted values are checked against your own master data and against internal consistency, such as line items reconciling to stated totals.
Escalation
Anything the checks cannot confirm is queued for a person, with the source document and the specific field in question shown side by side.
Export
Into SAP, your ERP, or Excel, using documented interfaces rather than database writes behind your vendor's back.
Your records
Extracted data is written to open formats as part of normal running, so your records always sit in files you control, independently of us.

Scope

It starts with one document type.

We do not attempt the whole back office at once. The first engagement takes a single high-volume document, proves the accuracy on your real paperwork, and earns the right to take the next one.

Accuracy on your documents

We run a sample batch of your actual paperwork and report what the system gets right, what it gets wrong, and what it refuses to guess. You see those numbers before committing.

Integration into a test system

Data is pushed into a sandbox copy of your system of record and reconciled against what your team would have entered by hand.

Live running and handover

Deployment with error logging, an escalation queue your staff own, and training. The people doing this work today become the people supervising it.

Deployment, now in scoping with a Malaysian trading company, starts with a single high-volume document type. What we build and harden for one operator is adapted for the next with the same problem. The next deployment is not an experiment: it arrives proven, and it arrives fast.

Beyond the filing

The paperwork is where it starts, not where it ends.

Once the documents flow through one pipeline, the work that follows them can ride the same rails: read, checked, and escalated to a person when unsure. Each is scoped the same way, one workflow at a time, against your real documents.

Supplier comparison
Quotations read and lined up on like terms, so choosing a vendor starts from the numbers instead of from whoever formatted their PDF best.
Drafted replies
Routine correspondence prepared from the documents at hand, waiting for a person to approve and send. Nothing leaves the building on its own.
Inventory signals
Stock estimates built from the sales and delivery history already passing through the pipeline, so purchasing sees what is running out before the shelf does.
Patterns over time
The documents accumulate into a record that can be asked questions: which supplier slips, which customer pays late, which product moves. Analysis on your data, inside your walls.

The staircase

You cannot put AI on paper.

You cannot skip a step. And AI cannot read a logbook in a drawer. Wherever your operation stands on the staircase, the pipeline starts there.
