For general audiences and associations

AI in everyday life: from chatting to autopilot

A general-audience talk on where AI already sits in everyday life, and what it can honestly do for a household or a business today.

Nobody in the room could tell a real photograph from a generated one. Yet everyone in it had already used AI a dozen times that day, unlocking a phone, rerouting around traffic, without once calling it AI. The talk starts from that gap.

The talk covers

  1. The AI you already use every day, without calling it AI
  2. Three levels of using it: ask, build a tool, autopilot
  3. Your expertise plus AI, from the kopitiam to the factory
  4. AI at home: health, family photos, a child's homework
  5. When AI is confidently wrong, and how to check it
  6. What does not need AI at all
Audience
Business owners, professionals and a general audience; no technical background assumed
Format
In-person talk with live audience participation
Level
General. If you have only ever heard of ChatGPT, that is enough

For engineers and technical teams

Engineering pragmatic AI systems, local architectures, and automated operational pipelines

For engineers, technical directors and operations heads who need the practical realities rather than the marketing.

Most enterprise failures do not come from the models. They come from integration friction: connecting messy operational data safely into legacy databases, holding the line on privacy, and proving the numbers are right.

The session covers

  1. Connecting messy operational data safely into legacy systems
  2. Running models on-premise instead of renting someone else's cloud
  3. The validation layers that decide whether a system may act
  4. Keeping security, compliance and explainable decisions intact
Audience
Professional engineers, technical directors, project managers, operations heads
Format
In-person technical session. 45-minute presentation followed by 15 minutes of questions
Level
Technical, but no machine learning background assumed
Built for
Professional bodies, engineering departments and in-house technical teams
