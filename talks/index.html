Current talk

Engineering pragmatic AI systems, local architectures, and automated operational pipelines

For professional engineers, technical directors, project managers and operations heads who need the architectural, security and practical realities rather than the marketing.

Artificial intelligence continues to dominate the headlines, but moving from a generic chat model to a reliable operational system remains the hurdle that traditional industries keep failing to clear. Most enterprise failures do not come from the models. They come from integration friction: connecting messy operational data safely into legacy databases, holding the line on data privacy, and proving that the numbers are right.

This session breaks down the engineering framework behind deploying AI in high-stakes environments. It covers data ingestion pipelines, running models on-premise instead of renting someone else's cloud, and the validation layers that decide whether a system is allowed to act. The through line is how to remove an operational bottleneck without giving up security, compliance, or the ability to explain a decision afterwards.

Previously

AI in everyday life: from chatting to autopilot

A general-audience talk on how one person runs a technology company with AI, and what that shift means for an established business.

The session opens by asking a room of people to tell real photographs from generated ones, and nobody can, including the person who builds these systems for a living. That is the honest starting point: the tools have crossed a line most people have not had time to notice, and pretending otherwise helps nobody.

From there it works through what has actually changed for operators and owners: the shift from chatting with AI to letting it do the work, what one person with agents can now run that used to take departments, which parts of a business move first, and what stays firmly human. No technical background is assumed at any point, and the room votes with raised hands throughout.
